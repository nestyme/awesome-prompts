# Evidence

Where the rules in SKILL.md come from. Two sources: our own negotiation log, and published creator-economy research. Where they disagree, both sides are listed at the end.

## Our log (Aug–Nov 2026)

A small consumer app (two founders) paying micro-creators a fixed fee per short video. Creators in Spain, Mexico, Chile, Colombia, Argentina, the US, Canada, the UK, Denmark, Korea and South Africa, contacted by cold email in their language, every message and outcome stored in a local CRM. The sample is small; treat the percentages as direction, not law, and recompute them on your own log.

**Replies**
- About a quarter of cold emails got a reply. Spanish-language emails got about twice the reply rate of English ones (≈46% vs ≈23%); German got none.
- 94% of replies came before any follow-up was sent. Follow-ups revived a few threads, mostly where the creator had simply missed the first email.
- A "we're a small family company" line in the intro did not raise reply rates (it was confounded with timing, so we can't say it hurt either). It did come back in creators' acceptance emails: they quoted it when agreeing to a lower number.

**Quotes and outcomes**
- Of creators who named a price, roughly a quarter ended in a signed deal.
- Most closed deals came from the lower half of the quotes we received. At the high end, deals closed only as a single "first test" video.
- When our offer (in practice, our ceiling) was at least ~60% of the creator's per-video ask, deals mostly closed: the creator accepted it or came back close to it. Under ~33%, almost none did. In between, scope trades (one test video, no cross-post) closed; pure price cuts stalled.
- Creators offered series discounts of typically 10–35% per video versus their single-video rate. Accepting the creator's own package price closed in all cases but one (the exception went silent before signing) and gave the lowest per-video prices in the whole log.
- Every closed deal settled within two rounds of counters.
- Agency, talent-manager and UGC-studio rate sheets (3–10× direct quotes, +30% posting, +30% usage rights) never closed.
- Most creators who declined on price replied warmly and asked us to come back when the budget grows.

**What the videos returned**
- A sponsored video got a median of about a third of the creator's unpinned median views (range ~15% to ~150%). Creators whose median came from one viral video got ~2%.
- Cost per thousand views varied more than 50× between deals. The cheapest: one fee covering TikTok, Reels and Shorts.
- Audience country decided payback. Videos with audiences in countries where the app doesn't sell brought installs and almost no subscribers (one: a burst of installs, none paid; another: a wave of users from Spain and LatAm, a small fraction of whom paid, far below the US rate).
- Per-25k-view bonuses on top of a small base rarely triggered at this scale.

**Process facts**
- Creators sent audience-by-country screenshots within a day when asked, with no pushback.
- Everyone who closed was paid upfront (in full, or per video before each one), via PayPal, fees covered. PayPal can't pay out in some currencies (COP, ZAR), so we paid USD at the day's rate.
- One LatAm creator's "$" turned out to mean local pesos, almost 20× less than the dollar reading. We asked before answering.
- A creator who wanted to negotiate on a call accepted email once we said we were writing through a translator.

## Market research (collected Nov 2026)

Vendor reports and surveys, mostly North American; treat as directional. ★ = non-obvious.

**Countering**
- Acknowledge the rate, give your budget with specifics, end with an open question; present the number as your limit, not their worth. [Partnrup](https://brands-user-guide.partnrup.ai/andreas-corner/what-to-do-when-a-creators-rate-comes-in-over-budget), [Superfiliate / Lydia Lee](https://www.superfiliate.com/field-guide/video/negotiation-levers-by-lydia-lee)
- ★ On deals of $500 or less: within 20% of the ask, accept without countering; further apart, 1–2 rounds then walk. [Partnrup](https://brands-user-guide.partnrup.ai/andreas-corner/what-to-do-when-a-creators-rate-comes-in-over-budget)
- Change scope rather than price; dropping exclusivity is the biggest lever, and usage rights can be skipped on a first collaboration. [Modash](https://www.modash.io/blog/negotiate-added-value), [Modash](https://modash.io/blog/influencer-negotiation)
- ★ Bundles meet little resistance (over 90% of marketers use them, 60% report no pushback); creators commonly offer ~20% off packs of 3+. [Modash](https://modash.io/blog/influencer-negotiation), [Karat](https://news.trykarat.com/p/brand-deal-negotiation)
- ★ Faster payment (net-15 or partial upfront) buys rate flexibility. [Superfiliate](https://superfiliate.com/field-guide/chapter/influencer-negotiation-levers-for-best-deals)
- ★ One in three marketers say renegotiating deliverables after agreement put a creator off entirely. [Modash](https://www.modash.io/blog/negotiate-added-value)

**Prices**
- ★ Collabstr 2026 (21k+ collaborations): ~80% of deals under $300; TikTok listed ~$182 vs paid ~$186, i.e. effectively no discount on marketplace prices. [Storyboard18](https://storyboard18.com/how-it-works/80-influencer-deals-under-300-brands-shift-to-ugc-report-92782.htm)
- Practitioners target 15–30% off cold quotes; 40%+ brings refusals or weaker content. [influencerfee.com](https://influencerfee.com/blog/instagram-influencer-negotiation-tips/)
- North American TikTok ranges differ 2–4× between sources (Modash $50–200 for 1–10k followers, $200–1,000 for 10–100k; Later notably higher). [Modash](https://www.modash.io/blog/influencer-pricing), [Later](https://later.com/blog/influencer-pricing-benchmarks-the-complete-2026-guide/)
- CPM figures range from ~$3 to $100+ depending on definition; compute your own. [Net Influencer on Later 2025](https://www.netinfluencer.com/brands-prioritize-revenue-over-reach-in-shift-to-performance-based-influencer-marketing-report-shows/)
- ★ LatAm creators often charge international brands more and adjust to the brand's reputation; nano creators often quote a round number with no method behind it. No credible public data shows LatAm CPMs below US ones. [HypeAuditor](https://blog.hypeauditor.com/how-foreign-brands-can-enter-latin-america-through-influencer-marketing/)

**Pay models**
- Most creators prefer a flat fee; performance pay works mainly on top of a flat base. ★ Creators' openness to affiliate deals fell from 63% to 26% in a year. ★ Nearly 75% of marketers who proposed minimum-view guarantees say it discouraged the creator. [Modash survey](https://www.modash.io/blog/survey-influencer-trends-contracts-negotiation-pricing), [Modash](https://modash.io/blog/influencer-negotiation)

**What creators resent**
- ★ 56% of creators have been paid late; 74% stopped working with a brand after feeling undervalued; creators want net-30 or faster, some charge late fees. [Digiday](https://digiday.com/marketing/in-a-booming-influencer-economy-creators-seek-standardization-for-payment-terms)
- Paid extras: usage rights and whitelisting each +30–50%, exclusivity at a premium, rush fees under two weeks, extra revisions; link in bio is a separate time-limited line. [Later](https://later.com/blog/influencer-pricing-benchmarks-the-complete-2026-guide/), [rate-card template](https://selenethelawyer.squarespace.com/blog/influencer-rate-card-template)
- Two rounds of minor edits is the norm; a reshoot is a new deliverable; unlimited revisions and late-added terms frustrate creators. [Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/influencer-collaborations-revision-limits/)
- Creators compare notes and rate transparency is spreading, so inconsistent offers get noticed. [Superfiliate / Milan Freeman](https://www.superfiliate.com/field-guide/video/how-to-and-how-not-to-negotiate-by-milan-freeman), [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/transparency-is-fueling-a-surge-in-creators-sponsorship-rates/)

**Language, region, payments**
- LatAm: write in Spanish (Portuguese for Brazil); ★ imperfect grammar is fine, the effort builds trust; deals often move to WhatsApp; partial or full prepayment is common and increasingly expected. [HypeAuditor](https://blog.hypeauditor.com/how-foreign-brands-can-enter-latin-america-through-influencer-marketing/)
- South Africa: PayPal can't hold ZAR; withdrawals via FNB add 0.81–1.51% on top of PayPal fees, so agree who covers fees. [FNB](https://www.fnb.co.za/forex/payments/paypal.html)
- Korea: since 2020 the regulator requires clear ad disclosure; vague labels such as "AD" or "Collaboration" don't count. [Asia Economy](https://cm.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2020062310093877218)

## Where the sources disagree

- **Follow-ups.** General cold-outreach studies say a follow-up doubles replies, and LatAm guides call several follow-ups normal. In our log 94% of replies came before any follow-up, LatAm included. Send one, with permission to say no, and stop.
- **Currency.** Common advice is to quote in local currency; HypeAuditor says many LatAm creators prefer USD. Mirror whatever the creator used.
- **Who names the number first.** Some negotiators say whoever names a number first loses. With a small fixed budget, asking for their rate first and then stating your one honest number saves rounds, and in our log a creator's self-set package price was the most reliable close.
- **Product as payment.** One vendor survey says most creators would work for product alone if they love the brand; creator communities reject gifted-only offers. For a digital product (an app subscription), treat access as an add-on only.
