# Whole-text rewrites

Sixteen rewrites across genres. Each shows the original, a short diagnosis by cause, the rewrite, and notes on the choices. Unlike the short pairs in `patterns.md`, these rewrites follow the real rule: every fact comes from the original, and anything missing goes into a `[placeholder]`. "Acme" and all the people here are made up.

Three things to notice across all of them:
1. **The rewrites are shorter**, often by half or more. Slop is padding, so removing it removes length.
2. **The emptier the original, the more placeholders.** When a text has no specifics, a good rewrite doesn't invent them. It returns a skeleton and tells the author exactly what to fill in. That's more useful than a polished paragraph of new fiction.
3. **Genre decides the form.** Release notes and READMEs keep their lists; an abstract stays formal; a toast gets short spoken sentences; fiction gets invented detail. The causes of slop are the same in all of them.

## 1. LinkedIn launch post

**Before**
> 🚀 Big news! I'm thrilled to announce that Acme has officially launched our AI-powered scheduling assistant!
>
> In today's fast-paced world, booking meetings shouldn't be a daily struggle. That's where Acme comes in.
>
> Acme isn't just another calendar app — it's your personal assistant, time manager, and productivity booster all in one.
>
> ✨ Smart scheduling that adapts to you
> ✨ Seamless integration with your favorite tools
> ✨ Powerful insights into how you spend your time
>
> This journey wouldn't have been possible without our incredible team, who worked tirelessly to bring this vision to life.
>
> The future of work is here — and it's personal.
>
> What's your biggest scheduling headache? Let me know in the comments! 👇

**What's going on**
- Template opener and closer (genre absorption): rocket emoji, "thrilled to announce", "In today's fast-paced world", "That's where Acme comes in", engagement-bait question.
- Insight-shaped filler (rewarded): "isn't just another X, it's Y" plus a triad of metaphors.
- Skim-optimized structure: emoji bullets, each a vague abstraction ("powerful insights").
- Inflation with no stake: "The future of work is here", "journey", "incredible team... tirelessly".
- Actual content: the scheduling assistant launched; it adapts to the user, connects to other tools, and shows where time goes; the team built it.

**After**
> Acme's scheduling assistant is live today.
>
> It finds meeting times and books them for you, [what "adapts to you" actually means, e.g. it learns which hours you keep free]. It connects to [which calendars and tools], and it shows you where your week went.
>
> Thanks to the team who built it: [names, with one concrete thing each person did].
>
> If you try it, tell me when it books something you'd never have accepted. That's what we're working on next.

**Notes**
- The headline fact goes first, with no emotional label.
- "Adapts to you" and "your favorite tools" are too vague to rewrite faithfully, so the author has to say what they mean. Guessing would be inventing features.
- Generic team thanks become a placeholder for specifics. Naming what people did is what makes thanks read as real.
- The closing question keeps its job (inviting replies) but becomes specific and answerable.

## 2. Cold sales email

**Before**
> Subject: Revolutionize Your Hiring Process with Acme! 🚀
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> I hope this email finds you well! I came across [Company] and was truly impressed by your innovative approach and impressive growth trajectory.
>
> I'm reaching out because Acme is a cutting-edge recruiting platform that empowers HR teams to streamline their hiring workflows, leverage AI-driven insights, and unlock top talent faster than ever before.
>
> Companies like yours have seen remarkable results, including reduced time-to-hire and improved candidate quality. Our seamless integration with your existing ATS ensures a frictionless transition.
>
> Would you be open to a quick 15-minute call next week to explore how Acme could help [Company] achieve its hiring goals?
>
> Looking forward to connecting!
>
> Best,
> Jordan

**What's going on**
- Chatbot residue and fake personalization: "I hope this email finds you well", "truly impressed by your innovative approach". Every recipient gets the same compliment, and they know it.
- The product is never described. "Streamline workflows, leverage insights, unlock talent" is a triad of abstractions that fits every HR tool on the market.
- "Remarkable results" with no number, from unnamed "companies like yours" (vague attribution).
- The only concrete things: it's recruiting software, it works with existing ATS systems, the ask is a 15-minute call.

**After**
> Subject: [Something specific to them, e.g. "Your 14 open engineering roles"]
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> [One specific, checkable reason you're writing now.]
>
> Acme is recruiting software that [what it does, in one sentence, e.g. "ranks incoming applications against the job description, so recruiters start with the strongest candidates"]. It works with the applicant tracking system you already use.
>
> [One real customer result with a number, e.g. time-to-hire before and after. If you don't have one, leave this out.]
>
> Worth 15 minutes next week?
>
> Jordan

**Notes**
- The original never says what the product does, so the rewrite can't either. The placeholder shows the author what kind of sentence is missing. This is usually the real problem with slop sales emails: the writer hasn't decided what the one-sentence pitch is.
- "Seamless integration ensures a frictionless transition" becomes the fact underneath it: it works with their ATS.
- A one-line ask replaces the call request wrapped in "explore how Acme could help achieve its goals".

## 3. Cover letter

**Before**
> Dear Hiring Manager,
>
> I am writing to express my keen interest in the Marketing Manager position at Acme. With a proven track record of driving results in fast-paced environments, I am confident that my unique blend of creativity, analytical skills, and strategic thinking makes me an ideal candidate for this role.
>
> Throughout my career, I have honed my ability to develop and execute innovative marketing strategies that resonate with target audiences. At my previous company, I spearheaded a social media campaign that significantly increased brand awareness and engagement. I am passionate about leveraging data-driven insights to craft compelling narratives that drive meaningful impact.
>
> I am particularly drawn to Acme's commitment to innovation and its dedication to empowering customers. I believe my skills and experience align perfectly with your team's goals, and I would welcome the opportunity to contribute to your continued success.
>
> Thank you for your time and consideration. I look forward to the possibility of discussing how I can contribute to your team.
>
> Sincerely,
> Priya Shah

**What's going on**
- Résumé clichés (most-probable phrasing): "proven track record", "fast-paced environments", "unique blend", "spearheaded", "passionate about leveraging data-driven insights".
- A triad of self-descriptions nobody can check (creativity, analytical skills, strategic thinking).
- "Commitment to innovation" is the reason given for wanting to work at Acme, which would fit any company.
- The only fact: she led a social media campaign at her previous company that raised awareness and engagement.

**After**
> Dear [hiring manager's name, if you can find it],
>
> I'd like to apply for the Marketing Manager role at Acme.
>
> At [previous company], I led a social media campaign that [the result, with a number: followers, sign-ups, sales]. [One more thing you did that matches the job description, with its result.]
>
> [Why Acme in particular, in one or two sentences: something about its product, customers, or a recent move that you actually have a view on.]
>
> I'd be glad to walk you through the campaign.
>
> Priya Shah

**Notes**
- Hiring managers skim cover letters for evidence. The one real fact in the original is kept and given a slot for its number, which is what makes it evidence.
- "Why this company" is the line that separates a real letter from a mass one, and only the applicant can write it.
- "Dear Hiring Manager" is fine when there's no name; a name is better.

## 4. Product description

**Before**
> Elevate your morning ritual with the Acme Burr Grinder — where precision meets passion. Crafted with meticulous attention to detail, this state-of-the-art grinder delivers a consistently perfect grind, unlocking the full, rich flavor of your favorite beans. Whether you're a seasoned barista or a casual coffee lover, its intuitive design and 40 customizable settings make it effortless to brew café-quality coffee at home. Sleek, durable, and whisper-quiet, it's not just a grinder — it's an experience.

**What's going on**
- Brochure register: elevate, ritual, meticulous, state-of-the-art, unlocking, rich, effortless, café-quality.
- "Where precision meets passion", "it's not just a grinder, it's an experience" (negative parallelism), universal reader, a triad of adjectives at the end.
- Facts: it's a burr grinder, it grinds consistently, it has 40 settings, and the copy claims it's durable and quiet.

**After**
> The Acme Burr Grinder has 40 grind settings, so you can set it for anything from espresso to French press [check that the range covers both]. Burr grinders cut beans to an even size, and an even grind is what keeps coffee from tasting bitter and sour in the same cup.
>
> - 40 grind settings
> - [Burr type and material]
> - [Noise level in decibels, if measured; the original says "whisper-quiet"]
> - [Hopper capacity, dimensions, warranty]

**Notes**
- One explanatory sentence (why even grinding matters) replaces "unlocking the full, rich flavor". It's common knowledge about burr grinders, so it isn't inventing.
- Product pages are scanned, so specs go in a list. "Durable" and "whisper-quiet" become slots for the facts that would prove them: a warranty and a decibel number.

## 5. Blog intro

**Before**
> In today's uncertain economic landscape, building an emergency fund has become more important than ever. But what exactly is an emergency fund, and how much should you save? In this comprehensive guide, we'll delve into everything you need to know — from setting realistic goals to choosing the right savings account. Whether you're just starting your financial journey or looking to strengthen your safety net, this guide has something for everyone. Let's dive in!

**What's going on**
- The full SEO intro template: throat-clearing opener, question hook, "comprehensive guide", "delve", a false range ("from... to..."), universal reader, "journey", "Let's dive in".
- Zero information. The reader still doesn't know what an emergency fund is or how big it should be.

**After**
> An emergency fund is money set aside for expenses you can't plan for: losing your job, a medical bill, a car repair. The usual rule of thumb is three to six months of essential expenses, kept somewhere you can get at quickly without risking a loss.
>
> This guide covers how to work out your own number and where to keep the money.

**Notes**
- The intro answers its own question immediately, which is what readers (and search engines) reward.
- The definition and the three-to-six-month rule are standard, so stating them isn't inventing. If the article recommends something different later, match it.
- One line of orientation is enough for a guide. Everything else was warm-up.

## 6. "About us" page

**Before**
> Founded in 2015, Acme Partners stands at the intersection of strategy and execution. Our team of seasoned professionals is dedicated to empowering organizations to navigate complex challenges and unlock sustainable growth. With a client-centric approach and a commitment to excellence, we deliver tailored solutions that drive measurable results. Our proven methodology, combined with deep industry expertise, has established us as a trusted partner to businesses across a wide range of sectors.

**What's going on**
- Almost pure inflation (no stake): "stands at the intersection", "seasoned professionals", "unlock sustainable growth", "commitment to excellence", "trusted partner".
- Every sentence would be true of every consulting firm in the world, which means it says nothing about this one.
- The only facts: founded in 2015, it's a consulting firm, it works across sectors.

**After**
> Acme Partners has been advising companies since 2015. [What you actually do, in one sentence, e.g. "We help mid-sized manufacturers cut inventory costs."] [Who your clients are: size, sector, and a recognizable name if you're allowed to say it.] [One result for one client, with a number.]

**Notes**
- A 75-word paragraph becomes one sentence plus placeholders. That's the correct outcome when the original was empty, not a failure of the rewrite.
- "Drive measurable results" is a promise to measure something. The placeholder asks for the measurement.

## 7. Investor update

**Before**
> This month has been a transformative period for Acme. We've made significant strides across multiple fronts, including product development, customer acquisition, and team building. Our customer base continued to grow, reflecting strong product-market fit, while our engagement metrics remained robust. Additionally, we've been laying the groundwork for several exciting initiatives that we believe will position us for long-term success. While challenges remain, we're confident in our ability to navigate them and are more energized than ever about the road ahead.

**What's going on**
- No numbers at all in an investor update, the one genre whose whole job is numbers.
- Inflation ("transformative", "significant strides"), a triad of fronts, an "-ing" tail asserting product-market fit, a generic challenges-and-outlook ending.
- To an experienced investor, vagueness in an update usually reads as bad news being hidden.

**After**
> **[Month] at Acme**
>
> Customers: [number], [+/-X%] month over month. [Retention or engagement metric]: [X].
> Revenue: [$X].
> Cash: [$X], [N] months of runway.
>
> What went well: [one thing, with a number].
> What didn't: [one thing, and what you're doing about it].
> Next month: [the "exciting initiatives", named].
> Where you can help: [specific intros, hires, or advice you need].

**Notes**
- Investor updates are scanned, so short labeled lines work here. This is formatting that serves the reader, not decoration.
- "While challenges remain" becomes "What didn't". Investors trust founders who report bad news plainly.
- Every line is a placeholder because the original had nothing to preserve. Tell the author so directly.

## 8. Customer support reply

**Before**
> Hi there! Thank you so much for reaching out, and I'm truly sorry to hear about the trouble you've been experiencing with the app. I completely understand how frustrating this must be. Rest assured, our team is working diligently to resolve this issue as quickly as possible. In the meantime, you may want to try logging out and logging back in, which could potentially help resolve the problem. If the issue persists, please don't hesitate to reach out to us again. We truly appreciate your patience and understanding!
>
> Best regards,
> The Acme Team

**What's going on**
- Chatbot residue and performed empathy: "truly sorry", "I completely understand how frustrating this must be", "Rest assured", "We truly appreciate your patience".
- A hedge stack on the only useful sentence: "may want to try... which could potentially help".
- Signed by "The Acme Team", so no person is accountable.

**After**
> Hi [Name],
>
> Sorry about [the problem, in their words]. It's a bug on our side, and we're fixing it [timeline, if known].
>
> In the meantime, try logging out and back in. If that doesn't fix it, reply here and [what you'll do next].
>
> [Agent's first name]
> Acme

**Notes**
- The apology names the problem, which shows someone read the message. That does more than any amount of "I understand".
- The workaround goes from a double hedge to a plain instruction. If it only sometimes works, say so honestly: "This fixes it for most people."
- "Reply here and [next step]" gives the customer a real path instead of "don't hesitate to reach out".

## 9. Personal essay

**Before**
> Moving to a new city was a transformative experience that taught me so much about myself. It wasn't just about changing my address — it was about rediscovering who I truly am. Navigating unfamiliar streets, building new connections, and embracing the unknown pushed me out of my comfort zone in ways I never imagined. Looking back, I realize that every challenge was an opportunity for growth. Ultimately, this journey reminded me that home isn't a place — it's a feeling.

**What's going on**
- Every sentence is an abstraction about the experience, and none of them is the experience. This is the most-probable-phrasing pull at full strength: these sentences fit anyone's move to any city.
- Two "not X, it's Y" reframes, a triad of gerunds, "journey", "growth", a moral ending.

**After**
This one can't be rewritten without the author. Return questions instead of prose:

> To rewrite this, I need the actual move. Could you tell me:
> 1. Which city, and roughly when?
> 2. One specific moment from the first weeks that was hard or strange.
> 3. One thing you do now that you didn't do before the move.
> 4. When did it start to feel like home, if it has? What was happening?

For illustration only, here is the shape a rewrite takes once the author answers. The details below are invented to show the form; in a real rewrite they come from the author.

> For my first three weeks in Berlin I didn't have a real conversation with anyone except the woman at the bakery, and only because I kept getting my order wrong. Then a guy from my building asked if I wanted to join his five-a-side football team. I'm bad at football. I've played every Thursday for two years.

**Notes**
- For personal writing, asking questions beats rewriting. A polished paragraph of invented memories would be worse than the original.
- The illustration ends on a fact ("every Thursday for two years") and lets it carry the meaning the original stated as a moral.

## 10. Fiction

**Before**
> The rain fell softly against the window as Elena sat in the dimly lit café, her fingers wrapped around a cup of coffee that had long since gone cold. A wave of nostalgia washed over her. She couldn't help but feel that this moment was a testament to everything she had lost. The air was thick with unspoken words, and somewhere in the distance, a clock ticked — a quiet reminder that time waits for no one. She took a deep breath. It was time to face the truth.

**What's going on**
- The most probable scene: rain on the window, a dim café, cold coffee, a ticking clock. Each image is the first one anyone would think of, which is why none of them registers.
- Named emotions instead of shown ones (no model of the reader): "a wave of nostalgia washed over her", "she couldn't help but feel".
- Significance inflation inside fiction: "a testament to everything she had lost".
- Clichés with an em dash reveal ("a quiet reminder that time waits for no one") and the stock beat "She took a deep breath. It was time to face the truth."

**After** (one possible direction; the specifics are suggestions for the author to replace)
> Elena's coffee had gone cold an hour ago, and the waiter had stopped asking. Across the street, the shop that used to be her father's had a new sign, white letters, a name she couldn't pronounce. She'd read it so many times it had stopped being a word.
>
> She paid, left too big a tip for one coffee, and crossed the road.

**Notes**
- Fiction is the one genre where inventing concrete detail is the job. But "what she had lost" is plot, and the author decides plot. The father's shop is a stand-in to show the technique: one specific loss, shown through something she can see. Say so when handing it back.
- The emotion is in what she does (reads the sign until it stops being a word, overtips, crosses the road), not in what she "couldn't help but feel".
- "It was time to face the truth" becomes an action that does the same narrative job.

## 11. README

**Before**
> # Acme CLI
>
> Welcome to Acme CLI! 🎉 Acme CLI is a powerful, flexible, and easy-to-use command-line tool that empowers developers to seamlessly manage their cloud infrastructure. Built with developer experience in mind, it leverages cutting-edge technologies to streamline your workflow and boost productivity. Whether you're a seasoned DevOps engineer or just getting started, Acme CLI has you covered!
>
> ## ✨ Key Features
> - **🚀 Blazing Fast:** Lightning-fast performance for all your operations
> - **🔧 Highly Configurable:** Customize every aspect to fit your needs
> - **🔒 Secure:** Enterprise-grade security you can trust

**What's going on**
- A triad of adjectives ("powerful, flexible, and easy-to-use") and brochure verbs (empowers, seamlessly, leverages, streamline) in a technical document.
- Emoji headings and inline-header bullets whose text restates the label ("Blazing Fast: Lightning-fast performance").
- A developer reading this still doesn't know what the tool does or how to run it.
- The facts: it's a CLI, it manages cloud infrastructure, it's configurable, and the author claims it's fast and secure.

**After**
> # Acme CLI
>
> Manage your cloud infrastructure from the terminal. [Which providers and resources it supports.]
>
> ```
> [one real example command and its output]
> ```
>
> ## Install
>
> ```
> [install command]
> ```
>
> ## Features
>
> - [Concrete operation, e.g. "Create and destroy servers from a YAML file"]
> - [Concrete operation]
> - Every option can be set in `[config file]` or by flag; see [link to the full reference].
> - [What "secure" means here, e.g. "Credentials are read from your OS keychain and never written to disk."]
> - [Speed claim only with a benchmark, e.g. "Applies a 50-server plan in about 8 seconds."]

**Notes**
- READMEs are reference documents, so headings, code blocks, and lists are right. What changes is that each item says what the tool does.
- An example command in the first screen does more than any adjective.
- "Blazing fast" and "enterprise-grade security" stay only if they become checkable.

## 12. Release notes

**Before**
> We're excited to introduce a brand-new way to organize your work! 🎉 With our latest update, you can now seamlessly filter your tasks by assignee, making it easier than ever to stay on top of what matters most. We've also made several performance improvements and bug fixes to ensure a smoother, more enjoyable experience. Update now to unlock the full potential of your workflow!

**After**
> What's new in [version]:
> - Filter tasks by assignee.
> - [Which screens got faster.]
> - Fixed [the bugs users reported most, in their words].

**Notes**
- Release notes are a list genre, so bullets are right.
- "Performance improvements and bug fixes" is the most skipped sentence in software. Naming one real fix ("Fixed: comments disappearing after you edit a task") tells users you heard them.
- The call to action goes: anyone reading release notes has already updated.

## 13. Academic abstract

**Before**
> This study delves into the multifaceted relationship between screen time and sleep quality among adolescents. By leveraging a comprehensive mixed-methods approach, we shed light on the intricate ways in which digital media consumption shapes nightly rest. Our findings underscore the pivotal role of evening device use, highlighting its profound impact on sleep duration. These insights pave the way for future research and offer valuable implications for parents, educators, and policymakers alike.

**What's going on**
- AI vocabulary cluster: delve, multifaceted, leveraging, comprehensive, shed light, intricate, underscore, pivotal, profound, pave the way.
- An abstract with no sample, no method details, and no findings. "Our findings underscore the pivotal role" is a finding-shaped sentence with no finding in it.

**After**
> We examined how screen use relates to sleep among adolescents aged [range] in [country]. We combined a survey of [N] participants with [N] semi-structured interviews [or sleep diaries, or actigraphy]. [Main finding with effect size, e.g. "Each additional hour of device use after 9 p.m. was associated with X fewer minutes of sleep."] [Second finding.] [One specific implication, and for whom.]

**Notes**
- The rewrite stays formal: first-person plural, precise terms ("semi-structured interviews"), no contractions. Formal register isn't the problem; empty sentences are.
- An abstract's job is method plus findings. Everything that isn't one of those went.

## 14. Wedding toast

**Before**
> Good evening, everyone! For those who don't know me, I'm Sam, the groom's best friend. It's truly an honor to stand here today and celebrate the beautiful union of Daniel and Ana.
>
> Daniel and I have been on quite the journey together. From late-night study sessions to unforgettable road trips, he has always been there for me — a true friend in every sense of the word.
>
> When Daniel met Ana, everything changed. Their love is a testament to the power of connection, kindness, and unwavering support. Together, they bring out the best in each other.
>
> So please raise your glasses to Daniel and Ana — may your love continue to grow stronger with each passing day. Cheers!

**What's going on**
- A false range hiding two real stories ("from late-night study sessions to unforgettable road trips"), neither of which gets told.
- Abstractions about the couple: "testament to the power of connection, kindness, and unwavering support" (inflation plus a triad).
- "Everything changed" with nothing shown changing.
- Facts: Sam is Daniel's best friend; they studied together and took road trips; Daniel married Ana.

**After**
Like the essay, a toast runs on stories only the speaker has. Return a skeleton and questions:

> Good evening. I'm Sam. Daniel and I met [where and when].
>
> [One story from the study sessions or the road trips, told as what actually happened. Funny is good; nothing his grandmother shouldn't hear.]
>
> [The moment you knew Ana was right for him: something you saw, not a conclusion.]
>
> To Daniel and Ana.

Questions for Sam: Which road trip, and what went wrong on it? What did Daniel do differently after he met Ana that you noticed?

**Notes**
- Toasts are heard, not read: short sentences, one story, under three minutes.
- One real story beats a list of qualities. "A true friend in every sense of the word" is what people say when they haven't picked the story yet.

## 15. Fundraising appeal

**Before**
> Dear Friend,
>
> As the holiday season approaches, we are reminded of the profound impact that generosity can have on our community. At Acme Food Bank, we are deeply committed to fighting hunger and empowering families to thrive.
>
> This year, the need has never been greater. Rising costs have left countless families struggling to put food on the table. Your gift — no matter the size — can make a meaningful difference in the lives of those who need it most.
>
> Together, we can build a brighter future where no one goes hungry. Please consider making a donation today.
>
> With heartfelt gratitude,
> The Acme Food Bank Team

**What's going on**
- Throat-clearing seasonal opener, "deeply committed", "empowering families to thrive" (vague abstraction).
- "The need has never been greater" and "countless families" with no number. Donors respond to specifics: how many, what a gift buys.
- A generic outlook ending ("a brighter future") and a hedged ask ("please consider").
- Facts: it's a food bank, rising costs have increased need, it's asking for a holiday donation.

**After**
> Dear [first name],
>
> [A number that shows the need, e.g. "This November we served 1,240 families, up from 900 a year ago."] Rising food prices are most of the reason.
>
> [What a specific amount buys, e.g. "$25 fills one family's shelves for a week."]
>
> You can give at [link]. [Deadline or matching gift, if there is one.]
>
> [Director's name]
> Acme Food Bank

**Notes**
- "Rising costs" is kept as the stated cause, now tied to a number.
- An appeal that names a price per family converts better than one about "meaningful difference", and it's more honest.
- A person signs it.

## 16. Slack message

**Before**
> Hey team! 👋 Just wanted to quickly circle back on the website redesign. I think it's important that we align on our key priorities moving forward. Let's make sure we're all on the same page regarding timelines, deliverables, and resource allocation. Happy to set up a quick sync if that would be helpful! Let me know your thoughts. 🙏

**What's going on**
- Corporate hedging around a request that's never stated: "circle back", "align on key priorities", "on the same page", a triad of topics, "happy to set up a quick sync", "let me know your thoughts".
- Nobody can act on it, so it'll get three emoji reactions and no decisions.

**After**
> Website redesign: can we lock scope and the launch date by Friday? My proposal: [pages in scope], launch on [date], [who builds it]. If nobody objects by Thursday, I'll book the designer.

**Notes**
- The rewrite contains a proposal and a deadline, which is what "align" was avoiding. If the author doesn't have a proposal yet, the honest version is "Can someone own the redesign scope? I need it by Friday to book the designer."
- A default action ("If nobody objects...") turns a discussion into a decision.
