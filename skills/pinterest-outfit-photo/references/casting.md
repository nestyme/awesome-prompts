# Casting

The rule, set by the art director after three rejected rounds:
- The people are slim, beautiful and confident, like fashion influencers on Pinterest.
- Vary the hair, the skin and the makeup.
- They should never look plain or "average"; that kills the desire to try the look on.

Describe each person in one sentence:
- age;
- ethnicity or skin tone;
- hair (colour, cut, styling);
- makeup;
- one personal styling cue.

Avoid the bare word "model". "Striking", "beautiful" and "a fashion influencer" plus concrete features work better.

## Personal jewelry vs outfit jewelry

The roster's personal cues (huggies, hoops, rings, piercings, chains, sunglasses, caps) are defaults, and the outfit's accessories always win:
- Drop any cue that would compete with them. If the outfit has earrings, don't describe the person's earrings; if it has sunglasses or a hat, drop the person's.
- A kept cue (a signet ring, a nose stud) counts as an extra and must stay small.
- If the outfit has a beanie or cap, pick a person whose hair still shows under it (a mullet, long hair, a bob, curls). Avoid people whose identity is a haircut that a hat would hide (a quiff, a high-top fade).
- Keep makeup and glasses.

## Spreading the roster

- Assign people in the shot-list table before writing prompts.
- Within one trend's 4 photos, use 4 different people.
- In a tab of 20 trends, a person can recur after ~6 trends, appears in at most 6 photos in total, and is on at most 2 covers.
- Match the person to the vibe without stereotyping. Pink hair suits grunge, y2k and party. A slick bun suits old money and tailoring. Glasses suit poetcore.
- Fit the casting to the gender of the outfit.
